# R2 — MENA Payment-Rail Availability Matrix

**Date compiled:** 2026-06-12
**Method:** every cell rests on a source fetched on 2026-06-12; primary sources (processor country lists, central-bank documents, IMF Article IV/AREAER materials) wherever available. Cells that could not be settled against a primary source are marked **UNKNOWN** or **partial/UNVERIFIED** — never guessed.

**Framing note:** as of June 2026, all three major LLM providers gate API throughput behind cumulative-payment tiers (OpenAI's five usage tiers starting at $5 of payments, Anthropic's four build tiers, Google's billing-linked tiers), so payment-rail access functions as a de facto second-order chokepoint on top of formal country-availability lists.

**MANDATORY CAVEAT (applies to every cell):** processor unavailability is *evidence of payment-chokepoint exposure*, **not** proof that LLM API payment fails from that country — developers routinely use workarounds (foreign legal entities, prepaid and virtual cards, resellers, aggregators). Conversely, processor presence does **not** guarantee that LLM providers accept cards issued in that country. This matrix maps the **rail layer**, not observed API-payment outcomes.

## Per-cell notes

1. **Stripe** — stripe.com/global lists ~50 supported countries; the only MENA entry is the **United Arab Emirates** (full support, direct dashboard registration). India and Indonesia are flagged "Preview" (the invite-only/preview tier); no MENA country appears at any status other than UAE. Algeria, Bahrain, Egypt, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Morocco, Oman, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Sudan, Syria, Tunisia, Yemen, Iran: absent. Source: https://stripe.com/global (accessed 2026-06-12). Archive: lookup attempted via archive.org availability API on 2026-06-12; API rate-limited (HTTP 429) — snapshot URL not confirmed; latest-capture lookup link: https://web.archive.org/web/2026*/stripe.com/global (unverified).

2. **PayPal functionality tiers** — PayPal's official "Country/Region of Residence and Available Services" legal page (last updated 2018-07-31, still the operative published list) sorts countries into three tiers. From our 19: *send-and-receive*: Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Morocco, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE. *Send-and-receive with Automatic Transfer* (balance force-swept to a linked **US** bank account or card — i.e., receiving requires US banking access): Algeria, Egypt. *Send-only* (can link a card and pay, cannot receive): Tunisia, Yemen. *Not listed anywhere* (no PayPal service): Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Palestine, Sudan, Syria, Iran. Source: https://www.paypal.com/ga/legalhub/residence-full?country.x=GA&locale.x=en_GA (accessed 2026-06-12). Caveat: page's stated update date is 2018; cross-checked against [3].

3. **PayPal payouts country/feature table** — PayPal Developer "Payouts Country & Feature Support" (page updated 2026-05-08) lists, from our 19, only: Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Morocco, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE — all as "Send, receive, and withdraw." Algeria, Egypt, Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Palestine, Sudan, Syria, Tunisia, Yemen, Iran: **not listed** (cannot receive payouts). Consistent with [2] except that Algeria/Egypt's "Automatic Transfer" tier does not surface in the payouts table — treated here as receive-constrained. Source: https://developer.paypal.com/docs/payouts/standard/reference/country-feature/ (accessed 2026-06-12). Archive: rate-limited as in [1]; lookup link https://web.archive.org/web/2026*/developer.paypal.com/docs/payouts/standard/reference/country-feature/ (unverified).

4. **Adyen** — Adyen's official docs list supported onboarding locations as: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Gibraltar, Greece, Guernsey, Hong Kong, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Singapore, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, UK (incl. Isle of Man & Jersey), US (incl. Puerto Rico). **No MENA jurisdiction appears**; corroborated by the per-country verification-requirements pages, which contain no MENA entries. Sources: https://docs.adyen.com/marketplaces/onboard-users and https://docs.adyen.com/platforms/verification-requirements/data-formats-per-country (both accessed 2026-06-12).

5. **Checkout.com** — the official Country Terms page (the contracting-entity list, i.e., where merchants can contract) covers: UK, France/EEA, **United Arab Emirates** (Checkout MENA FZ-LLC, regulated by the Central Bank of the UAE), New Zealand, Hong Kong, Australia, Singapore, Canada, Japan, US. No other MENA jurisdiction has country terms. Checkout.com's support documentation describes "Middle East, North Africa, and Pakistan" KYC document requirements that name UAE and Saudi Arabia specifically, implying onboarding activity beyond UAE, but publishes no country-by-country merchant-eligibility list — hence **UNKNOWN** for BH/EG/IQ/JO/KW/LB/MA/OM/PS/QA/TN rather than a guessed No, and **No** only where sanctions or absence of any operating evidence make it settled (DZ, LY, SD, SY, YE, IR). Sources: https://www.checkout.com/legal/country-terms ; https://support.checkout.com/hc/en-us/articles/15260321213330-Verify-individuals (both accessed 2026-06-12).

6. **Algeria cards** — Bank of Algeria Instruction No. 05-2025 (2025-07-17, effective 2025-07-20) sets the entire personal FX regime as a once-yearly cash *droit de change pour voyage* (€750 adults / €300 minors, ≥7-day stay required); December 2025 BA notes to banks tightened administration further (scriptural payment of the dinar countervalue via domestic CIB card/cheque; mandatory bank account; clawback for stays <7 days). There is no provision for international online payment with domestic dinar cards; the CIB card is a domestic-network instrument. Sources (paraphrasing BA instructions as reported): https://algerie-eco.com/2025/07/17/banque-dalgerie-la-nouvelle-allocation-touristique-en-vigueur-a-compter-du-20-juillet/ ; https://algerie-eco.com/2025/12/17/allocation-touristique-la-banque-dalgerie-precise-les-modalites-de-reglement-de-la-contre-valeur-en-dinars/ (accessed 2026-06-12). Status: regime documented from BA instrument numbers via Algerian financial press — **verified-secondary on primary instruments**.

7. **Algeria FX severity** — IMF, *Algeria: 2025 Article IV Consultation* (Country Report 25/270, 2025-09-18): parallel-market premium widened from 50% to 70% over two years; "tight restrictions on some imports and some FX transactions"; staff urges BA to "ensure that sufficient foreign exchange is available for current international transactions, in line with IMF Article VIII obligations." Source: https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1dzaea2025001-source-pdf.pdf (accessed 2026-06-12).

8. **Bahrain** — Article VIII member with an open exchange system and USD peg; the 2025 Article IV (Board conclusion 2026-01-27) flags fiscal, not exchange, vulnerabilities; no exchange restrictions reported. Source: https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/27/pr-26023-the-kingdom-of-bahrain-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation (accessed 2026-06-12). Card cell rests on the absence of any documented restriction in IMF/CBB materials — classified Yes with that basis stated.

9. **Egypt cards** — CBE circular of 2025-08-12 cancelled the requirement (introduced Oct 2023) that customers prove physical presence abroad (entry/exit stamps within 90 days) for foreign card use; banks retain monitoring rights per the 2023-10-29 and 2024-02-11 circulars. Simultaneously, major banks (NBE, CIB, Banque Misr, AAIB) cut FX markup fees 5%→3% and raised overseas-use limits to ≈$10,000. Residual bank-level limits and FCY-merchant restrictions remain. Sources: CBE circular as logged by RegAlert: https://regalert.today/document/0b93a3be-89f7-46a8-af64-7c4cfcadb440 ; https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/551158.aspx ; https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/551185.aspx (accessed 2026-06-12). Status: circular existence primary-adjacent (regulatory tracker), limit figures **verified-secondary**.

10. **Egypt FX severity** — IMF, Egypt 2025 Article IV / Fourth EFF Review — Informational Annex (Country Report 25/186, Jul 2025): Egypt "has accepted the obligations of Article VIII... and maintains an exchange system that is free of restrictions on the making of payments and transfers for current international transactions"; de jure and de facto floating since March 2024; parallel-market gap closed. Classified *moderate* (not none) given the 2022–2025 history of administrative card limits ([9]) and the narrow trading range staff flag. Source: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2025/186/article-A002-en.xml (accessed 2026-06-12).

11. **Iraq** — Central Bank of Iraq circulars No. 14/8/1165 and 14/8/993 (as implemented by banks, April 2025) set monthly ceilings for locally issued cards used abroad: $5,000/month general (ATM, POS, online); $10,000 retirees; $20,000 travel-MCC-restricted; $50,000 medical; new cards activate for international use 30 days after issuance; usage tied to authorized MCC lists. CBI's "third package" (2023) had earlier set the official-rate card channel (prepaid cap $10,000) as the sanctioned route for personal FX. International use is therefore possible but capped and MCC-gated. Sources: BBAC Iraq (bank implementation page citing CBI circular numbers): https://bbacbank.com.iq/Service/104/186/Cards/Updated-Monthly-Limits-for-Card-Usage-Abroad ; CBI: https://cbi.iq/news/print_news/2276 ; corroboration: https://baghdadtelegraph.com/iraqs-central-bank-sets-new-limits-for-card-use-abroad/ (accessed 2026-06-12). Note: one secondary analysis (rawabetcenter.com, Apr 2025) describes a stricter travel/medical-only $2,000/month rule; the bank-published circular numbers above are treated as controlling, discrepancy flagged.

12. **Jordan** — Article VIII member; JOD pegged to USD; IMF EFF program ongoing (2024 Art IV / Second Review, Country Report 24/345). No exchange restrictions on current transactions documented; card cell = Yes on that basis. Source: https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1jorea2024003-print-pdf.pdf (accessed 2026-06-12).

13. **Kuwait** — IMF Informational Annex (2019, reaffirmed by subsequent Art IVs incl. 2025, Country Report 26/052): Kuwait "has accepted the obligations of Article VIII... and maintains an exchange system free of restrictions on payments and transfers for current international transactions," other than UNSC-related notifications. Sources: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2019/095/article-A002-en.xml ; https://www.imf.org/en/publications/cr/issues/2026/02/24/kuwait-2025-article-iv-consultation-press-release-and-staff-report-574216 (accessed 2026-06-12).

14. **Lebanon** — Banque du Liban Basic Decision No. 13729 (2025-07-01) prohibits banks from paying out of pre-17-Nov-2019 foreign-currency accounts beyond BdL-prescribed limits without prior BdL approval — the first regulatory codification of the informal controls in place since Oct 2019. Cards funded by "fresh dollar" (post-2019, externally funded) accounts regained international/online function from ~2022; pre-crisis accounts remain unusable for cross-border payment. Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/uk/financial-services/1662790/ (analysis of BD 13729, Arabic/French original at BdL); https://www.lcps-lebanon.org/en/articles/details/5004/central-bank-circulars-and-deposit-access-in-2025 ; https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/lebanon-trade-financing ; US State Dept 2024 Investment Climate Statement: https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2024-investment-climate-statements/lebanon/ (accessed 2026-06-12).

15. **Libya** — Central Bank of Libya Circular No. 10 of 2025 (2025-04-06): annual personal-FX purchase limit cut from $4,000 to **$2,000**, usable via prepaid cards, Visa/Mastercard payments, or international transfer; students $7,500/yr; medical $10,000/yr; commercial prepaid cards for traders capped at $50,000. CBL's own FX-use statistics (Jan–Dec 2025 report, cbl.gov.ly) show "personal purposes" and "merchants' cards" as administered FX categories. International card use thus exists only inside the CBL quota system. Sources: https://cbl.gov.ly/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2026/01/Uses-of-Fx-from-1-Jan-to-31-December-2025.pdf (primary); circular contents: https://sada.ly/en/exclusive-including-2000-personal-use-cards-central-bank-issues-new-regulations-for-foreign-currency-sales/ and Zahaf & Partners summary of Circular 10/2025 (LinkedIn, 2025-04-08) (accessed 2026-06-12).

16. **Morocco** — Office des Changes, Instruction Générale des Opérations de Change (IGOC) 2026, Articles 120–131 (in force 2026-01-01): international e-commerce payable by Moroccan-bank-issued international cards within annual dotations — 20,000 MAD/yr for resident individuals (raised from 15,000), up to 2M MAD for ADD-labelled startups, and 50,000 MAD minimum up to 5M MAD for companies (indexed to corporate tax paid). Transactions auto-reported to the Office des Changes; crypto and gambling excluded. Sources: http://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/commerce-electronique-a-international (primary); corroboration: https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/ecommerce-exchange-control-morocco/ ; https://fr.le360.ma/economie/reglementation-des-changes-dotation-pour-voyages-e-commerce-export-import-ce-qui-change-des-janvier_BPDQLTUSQFE5PNTNKVZO3UMNA4/ (accessed 2026-06-12).

17. **Oman** — IMF, Oman 2024 Article IV — Informational Annex (Country Report 25/013): Article VIII member; "maintains an exchange system free of multiple currency practices and restrictions on the making of payments and transfers for current international transactions" (other than Decision 144-notified security measures). Source: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2025/013/article-A002-en.xml (accessed 2026-06-12).

18. **Palestine** — 7amleh, *Palestinian Access to Digital Economy Platforms* (report released 2026-05-05): PayPal, Payoneer, Stripe, Wise exclude or severely limit West Bank/Gaza residents while serving Israeli citizens including settlers; Palestinian banks issue Visa/Mastercard, participate in SWIFT, support Apple Pay, and the PMA maintains AML standards — i.e., locally issued cards function internationally in principle, with correspondent-banking and political friction in practice. Card cell marked **partial/UNVERIFIED** because no PMA primary rule-text was fetched; the functional claim rests on 7amleh's platform-testing methodology. Sources: https://7amleh.org/storage/posts/pdf/7617f280-48ed-4881-80bf-b9d215efdb8a.pdf ; https://7amleh.org/post/palestinian-exclusion-from-the-digital-economy-en ; https://p4p.7amleh.org/about (accessed 2026-06-12).

19. **Qatar** — IMF, Qatar 2024 Article IV (Country Report 25/47, Jan 2025): peg "continues to serve Qatar well," ample buffers; Article VIII member; no exchange restrictions reported. Source: https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1qatea2025001-print-pdf.pdf (accessed 2026-06-12).

20. **Checkout.com in Saudi Arabia** — serving KSA merchants since 2014, Riyadh office, first PSP to acquire mada online (2018, SAMA-demonstrated), active KSA enterprise merchant announcements through 2026-06-03 (Nahdi). However, the country-terms page lists no Saudi contracting entity (GCC merchants appear to contract via Checkout MENA FZ-LLC, UAE). Hence "partial/UNVERIFIED" for formal merchant-country status. Sources: https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/checkout-com-and-nahdi-partner-to-drive-digital-payment-performance-in-saudi-and-across-the-gcc ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/checkoutcom-becomes-first-payment-solutions-provider-to-accept-payments-via-mada-saudi-arabias-domestic-payment-network-680209693.html ; https://www.checkout.com/legal/country-terms (accessed 2026-06-12).

21. **Saudi Arabia FX** — US ITA Country Commercial Guide (official US-government source drawing on SAMA rules): "There is no limitation in Saudi Arabia on the inflow or outflow of funds"; SAR pegged at 3.75/USD since 1986. Article VIII member. Source: https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/saudi-arabia-trade-financing (accessed 2026-06-12).

22. **Tunisia** — Tunisian Customs (douane.gov.tn, official): allocation touristique capped at 6,000 TND per calendar year per resident, delivered against authorization valid one month. US ITA Tunisia eCommerce guide: "Tunisian credit cards are not approved for transactions in currencies other than the dinar and therefore cannot be used for purchases made on foreign commercial internet sites"; exception: the government "digital technology charge card" (1,000 TND/yr individuals; 10,000 TND/yr IT companies; up to 100,000 TND for Startup-Act-qualified firms). BCT circulars (e.g., n°2007-04 and successors) govern the allocation regime. Sources: https://www.douane.gov.tn/exportation-de-devises/ (primary); https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/tunisia-ecommerce ; https://www.bct.gov.tn/bct/siteprod/index.jsp?la=AN (accessed 2026-06-12). Note: the ITA card-restriction text predates 2026 and BCT has periodically adjusted ceilings — treated as **partial/UNVERIFIED for current ceilings**, structurally confirmed.

23. **UAE** — IMF, UAE 2022 Article IV — Informational Annex (reaffirmed 2024, 2025 Art IVs): Article VIII accepted Feb 1974; "maintains an exchange system that is free of restrictions on the making of payments and transfer for current international transactions and multiple currency practices," save Decision 144-notified security measures. Sources: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2023/223/article-A002-en.xml ; https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1areea2025001-source-pdf.pdf (accessed 2026-06-12).

24. **Yemen** — IMF, 2025 Article IV mission statement (2025-10-09) and Board conclusion (2026-04-02, Country Report 26/080): 2025 measures include limits on foreign-currency exchanges, prohibition of FCY use in local transactions, import-FX channeling via the NCRFI; staff note further assessment needed on Article VIII consistency; reserves ≈ one month of imports. No primary CBY rule on card use abroad located — card cell **UNKNOWN**. Sources: https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2025/10/09/imf-cs-yemen-2025-imf-article-iv-mission ; https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/02/pr-26106-yemen-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consult (accessed 2026-06-12).

25. **Syria** — OFAC FAQs (2025-06-30): comprehensive US sanctions lifted effective 2025-07-01; Syrian financial institutions including the central bank removed from the SDN List; correspondent banking permitted. Central Bank of Syria decision of 2026-05-04 authorizes licensed banks/payment companies to work with international card schemes; first Visa/Mastercard test transactions processed 2026-05-09 via the new Paymera network — a trial, not general availability; Syria remains on the FATF grey list and analysts note USD/EUR receiving remains largely impractical. Sources: https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/added/2025-06-30 (primary); https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2026/05/syrias-central-bank-allows-banks-to-work-with-global-payment-companies/ ; https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2026/05/syria-tests-visa-and-mastercard-payments-after-15-years/ ; https://gfmag.com/news/visa-mastercard-restart-business-syria/ (accessed 2026-06-12).

26. **Sudan** — IMF September 2025 assessment (as reported): "comprehensive monetary collapse," pound no longer functioning reliably; parallel rate ~3,700 vs official ~2,600–2,700 SDG/USD (Oct 2025); >80% of transactions in dollars or barter. World Bank Sudan Economic Update (2025): official/parallel gap re-opened post-April-2023, central bank FX rationing. No functioning rule-based card-abroad channel documented; cells **No/UNVERIFIED**. Sources: https://thesudantimes.com/sudan/imf-warns-sudan-faces-total-monetary-collapse-and-dual-recession/ (secondary on IMF note); https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051925180542315/pdf/P178527-6a7d4d70-52be-49b7-87e5-ead89050a99b.pdf (primary) (accessed 2026-06-12).

27. **Iran** — sanctions sever Iranian banking from Visa/Mastercard/Amex and SWIFT (central bank sanctioned 2011–12; OFAC Iran program current per Jan 2026 documentation); domestic Shaparak/Shetab networks operate in isolation; foreign cards do not work in Iran and Iranian cards do not work abroad; CBI issues rial tourist debit cards precisely because international schemes are absent. Sources: https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/inside-irans-payments-system ; https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/511724/Iran-s-Central-Bank-introduces-rial-debit-cards-for-tourists (accessed 2026-06-12).

## Limitations

1. **Rail layer only (mandatory caveat restated).** Processor unavailability here is evidence of payment-chokepoint exposure, not proof that LLM API payment fails from that country: developers use foreign entities, prepaid/virtual cards, and resellers; and processor presence does not guarantee that OpenAI/Anthropic/Google billing systems accept cards issued in that country. No cell in this matrix records an observed API-payment outcome.
2. **Archive snapshots not confirmed.** The Internet Archive availability API returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited) on all attempts on 2026-06-12. A Save Page Now retry later the same day (Phase 2/3 fixes pass) also failed: web.archive.org/save was unreachable (HTTP 000, connection-level failure) for all five queued URLs (Stripe global, Mistral ToS, Azure account FAQ, AWS contracting-party, OpenRouter privacy). No snapshot URLs are asserted; the lookup-pattern links in notes [1] and [3] are provided for later verification, not as evidence. **Retry again at Phase 4 QA before ship; the brief's Sources section claims snapshots only for the pages actually pinned (Phase 0/V5 core country lists).**
3. **PayPal legal page is dated 2018.** The residence-services tiering page (S2) carries a 2018 update stamp. It remains PayPal's operative published list and was cross-checked against the May 2026 payouts table (S3); where the two diverge (Algeria, Egypt "Automatic Transfer" tier vs. absence from payouts), both are recorded and the cell is marked partial.
4. **Checkout.com publishes no merchant-country list.** Its eligibility documentation is industry-based, and country terms exist only for its contracting entities. GCC-and-wider-MENA availability outside UAE could not be settled from primary documents — hence the UNKNOWN cells, despite visible commercial operations in KSA.
5. **Card-abroad rules churn quickly.** Egypt (Aug 2025 easing), Libya (April 2025 quota cut from $4,000 to $2,000), Algeria (Jul/Dec 2025 tightening), and Iraq (April 2025 ceilings) all changed within the past 14 months; figures here are point-in-time as of 2026-06-12. The Tunisia card-restriction text from trade.gov predates 2026 and current ceilings are unverified.
6. **AREAER not directly queried.** The IMF AREAER online database sits behind a login; Article VIII / restriction statements were instead taken from the most recent published Article IV informational annexes and staff reports, which restate AREAER-jurisdiction findings. A subscription pull of AREAER 2025 country chapters would strengthen rows DZ, IQ, LB, LY, SD, SY, YE, IR.
7. **Several primary instruments were reached through reputable carriers** (Algerian financial press for BA instructions; RegAlert for the CBE circular; a law-firm summary for CBL Circular 10/2025; Mondaq/Herbert Smith Freehills for BdL BD 13729). Instrument numbers and dates are cited so the originals can be pulled from the issuing authorities.
8. **Sanctions-group volatility.** Syria's status is in active transition (card-scheme reconnection began May 2026); any cell in that row may be stale within months.
