## Method note

**Verification levels:**

- **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** — official provider/operator statement (status page, company announcement, regulator decision) or institutional report (NetBlocks, Cloudflare Radar, Internet Society Pulse, EUNAVFOR, MARAD, OFAC).
- **VERIFIED-SECONDARY** — at least two independent reputable press accounts that agree on the core facts.
- **UNVERIFIED** — neither of the above. Not included in the main table; listed under "Leads not substantiated."

**Policy mechanisms vs. realized incidents.** This file distinguishes (a) *realized incidents* — dated interruptions of flow that actually occurred — from (b) *standing policy mechanisms* — official gates (country lists, payment rules, ownership rules) that condition access continuously. Per the work order: **no verified MENA-specific LLM-service interruption incident was found at the payment/policy layer; the official policy mechanisms documented below therefore serve as proof-of-mechanism for the brief's MENA exposure argument.** What *was* verified for MENA geography: (1) an infrastructure incident (Sept 2025 Red Sea cable cuts) that degraded cloud/API transit through the region, (2) a conflict-driven national connectivity collapse (Sudan 2023–24) that severed all API access as a side effect, and (3) a documented period in which Egypt (and other Arab countries) were excluded from official ChatGPT availability (launch through Nov 2023) — a gate, not an interruption of existing flow.

All URLs accessed 2026-06-12 unless otherwise noted. Sources are paraphrased throughout.

## Red Sea, September 6, 2025 — citation-check result (pre-sourced entry)

Task: confirm the three claimed source types (NetBlocks, Azure status page, Network World / Windows Central) actually document the event. **Result: all three source types check out, with three flags.**

1. **NetBlocks — CONFIRMED.** The actual post exists: NetBlocks published on September 6, 2025 (X and Mastodon) confirming a series of subsea cable outages degrading connectivity in multiple countries including Pakistan and India, attributed to failures affecting the SMW4 and IMEWE systems near Jeddah. Durable URL: https://mastodon.social/@netblocks/115159545022674516 (accessed 2026-06-12). The X version is quoted verbatim by Indian Express and Sky News. **Flag 1:** NetBlocks named only SMW4 and IMEWE. FALCON GCX was identified by Kuwaiti authorities (reported by AP/Euronews), and Kentik's Doug Madory later added EIG. The brief should not attribute the FALCON GCX identification to NetBlocks.

2. **Microsoft Azure status page — CONFIRMED in substance, with an archival caveat.** Microsoft did post the notice ("Starting at 05:45 UTC on 06 September 2025, network traffic traversing through the Middle East may experience increased latency due to undersea fiber cuts in the Red Sea…"), confirmed verbatim by Reuters/CNBC, BBC, The Register, and Network World. **Flag 2:** the live Azure status history page (https://azure.status.microsoft/en-us/status/history/) no longer lists the September 2025 incident (checked 2026-06-12 — history now begins April 2026). A live-link citation would dangle. Use the archived incident text — full verbatim Azure status records are preserved at https://isdown.app/status/azure/incidents/441417-multiple-subsea-fiber-cuts-in-the-red-sea-impacting-global-communications (initial notice) and https://isdown.app/status/azure/incidents/443403-increased-network-latency-on-traffic-routes-through-the-middle-east (Sept 9–10 wind-down notice) — or cite CNBC/Reuters quoting the status page. (Wayback Machine API returned 429 rate-limit errors throughout this session, so a web.archive.org snapshot could not be confirmed; the IsDown mirrors carry the full status text.)

3. **Press coverage — CONFIRMED.** Network World, "Red Sea cable cuts trigger latency for Azure, cloud services across Asia and the Middle East" (2025-09-08): https://www.networkworld.com/article/4052813/red-sea-cable-cuts-trigger-latency-for-azure-cloud-services-across-asia-and-the-middle-east.html — confirms the 05:45 UTC start, the rerouting, and the status-page quotes. Windows Central, "Red Sea cable cuts disrupt 17% of traffic, Microsoft reacts" (2025-09-09): https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/azure/microsoft-links-red-sea-cable-cuts-to-global-internet-slowdown — exists and covers the event. Additional strong accounts: CNBC (2025-09-06), BBC (2025-09-07), The Register (2025-09-08).

4. **Flag 3 — the "~17%" figure.** Institutional sourcing supports "approximately 17% of global internet traffic transits the Red Sea corridor" (corridor-share figure, reported by The National citing the corridor's role, and widely repeated). It does **not** support "17% of intercontinental traffic was affected/disrupted" — the affected share was never quantified in any source found. The Windows Central headline uses the looser framing. Recommend the brief phrase it as corridor share, not affected share.

Verification level for the entry as a whole: **VERIFIED-PRIMARY** (operator status text + NetBlocks institutional monitoring + multiple independent press).

## Red Sea / Houthi resumption, ~February 28, 2026 — verification result (for T12)

**Question:** did Houthi/Red Sea attacks on shipping resume on/around February 28, 2026?

**Verdict: split — announcement VERIFIED; actual resumed attacks on shipping UNVERIFIED for that date (and contradicted by institutional sources through at least late March 2026).**

- **VERIFIED:** On February 28, 2026, following US-Israeli strikes on Iran, two senior Houthi officials told international media (Reuters; AP) that the group had decided to resume missile and drone attacks on Red Sea shipping routes and on Israel, with attacks possible imminently. Multiple wires and trade press carried it the same day. Sources: Reuters via The Hindu (2026-02-28): https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/yemens-iranian-backed-houthi-rebels-to-resume-attacks-on-shipping-in-red-sea-corridor-officials/article70687579.ece ; gCaptain (2026-02-28): https://gcaptain.com/houthis-signal-renewed-red-sea-shipping-attacks-after-u-s-israeli-strikes-on-iran/ ; FreightWaves (2026-02-28): https://www.freightwaves.com/news/iran-backed-houthi-warn-of-new-red-sea-attacks (all accessed 2026-06-12).
- **NOT SUBSTANTIATED:** that merchant vessels were actually attacked on/around February 28, 2026. Institutional sources are explicit: EUNAVFOR ASPIDES's informational message of March 28, 2026 records the last merchant-vessel incident as September 29, 2025 and "days with no incident: 190," noting the Houthis resumed missile fire against Israel on March 28, 2026 — not against shipping (https://mscio.eu/media/documents/20260328-EUNAVFOR_ASPIDES_Informational_Message_to_the_Shipping_Industry_mod.pdf). UKMTO kept the Red Sea threat level "moderate" through late March, with no confirmed systematic resumption against maritime traffic. US MARAD advisory 2026-006 (https://www.maritime.dot.gov/msci/2026-006-red-sea-bab-el-mandeb-strait-gulf-aden-arabian-sea-and-somali-basin-houthi-attacks) treats the threat as active but documents no post-Feb-28 merchant strike. gCaptain (2026-03-02) likewise: no confirmed new maritime strike independently verified.
- **Flow-relevant commercial effects did occur:** Maersk withdrew some Red Sea voyages around February 27–28, 2026, and operators elevated threat protocols (FreightWaves; gCaptain). Threat signaling alone moved routing.

**Citable formulation for the brief:** "On 28 February 2026 the Houthi leadership announced it would resume attacks on Red Sea shipping; carriers immediately began withdrawing voyages, though no confirmed strike on a merchant vessel had been independently verified through late March 2026." Do not write "attacks resumed on Feb 28."
